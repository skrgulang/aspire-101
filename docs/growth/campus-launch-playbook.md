# Aspire 101 campus launch playbook

## Goal

Do not optimize for raw signups. A campus is "working" when students can reliably post a need/listing and receive a useful response fast enough to return.

## Launch metrics

Track weekly:

- verified student signups;
- activated users who create a request/listing or send a response;
- number of open requests/listings;
- median time to first response;
- request → response conversion;
- response → connection conversion;
- completed connections/orders;
- 7-day returning users;
- safety/support incidents per 100 active users.

## Pilot target

For one pilot campus, aim for an initial operating pool such as:

- 25–50 verified students;
- 10+ useful live requests/listings in the first week;
- first response to most viable requests within 24 hours;
- at least 5 completed connections/orders before expanding promotion.

These are operating targets, not promises.

## Supply before promotion

Seed useful, normal student categories before broad marketing:

- study/accountability help;
- campus errands and practical help;
- dorm/move-in/move-out needs;
- event/community help;
- safe secondhand marketplace listings.

Avoid empty-category expansion. It is better to make two categories feel alive than ten categories feel abandoned.

## 14-day launch sequence

### Days -7 to 0: seed

- Recruit a small core of ambassadors/builders and trusted early users.
- Create real requests/listings; no fake activity.
- Test onboarding, response, chat, completion, reporting, and support.
- Prepare QR/link assets with UTM tags by channel.

### Days 1–3: concentrated launch

- One campus, a few high-density channels: student orgs, residence communities, relevant Discord/GroupMe channels, tabling, and direct referrals.
- Ambassador/demo content should show one concrete job-to-be-done rather than a generic "new app" pitch.
- Founder/support watches the first responses and fixes friction quickly.

### Days 4–7: liquidity review

- Identify categories with requests but no responses.
- Recruit supply specifically for those categories.
- Contact early users for short qualitative feedback.
- Remove confusing or low-trust acquisition copy.

### Days 8–14: retention

- Encourage completed users to post/respond again.
- Test referral prompts only after a successful experience.
- Publish useful campus-specific examples and social proof without exposing private user information.
- Decide whether the campus has enough repeat activity to widen marketing.

## Channel measurement

Use GA4/UTM for acquisition source, Amplitude for activation/retention, and Supabase/financial records for real completion/revenue. Do not infer marketplace revenue from browser analytics alone.

## Expansion gate

Do not open a second major campus push until the first pilot shows repeat behavior and acceptable response times. Growth without local liquidity creates an empty-marketplace problem faster.
